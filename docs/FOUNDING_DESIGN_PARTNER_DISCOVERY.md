# Founding Design-Partner Discovery Guide

**Status:** Discovery template — planning only. No contact has been made with any
prospective customer as part of creating this document.

**Purpose:** Give a repeatable, neutral way to learn — from a small number of
founding design partners — which MYReSolve outcomes leaders value enough to adopt
or pay for, which trust concerns block adoption, and which two or three data
connections matter most, *before* product code, authentication, or billing
expands.

**Reference basis (read-only):** `docs/SUBSCRIPTION_MVP_BRIEF.md`,
`docs/SECURE_SUBSCRIPTION_PILOT_PLAN.md`, `docs/SECURITY_DATA_BLUEPRINT.md`,
`docs/EXTERNAL_FEEDBACK_001.md`, and `ROADMAP.md`. This guide must not contradict
those documents or the locked assessment scoring model.

---

## 1. How to use this guide

- Run one conversation at a time, about 30–35 minutes, with a single leader.
- Ask open questions, listen more than you talk, and capture the meaning of what
  the participant says in your own paraphrase — not distinctive verbatim quotes
  (see §6).
- In your **private notes only**, work against coded labels (**Partner A, B,
  C…**), never a real name. Those labels must **never enter a committed file** —
  a label reused across themes can re-link one participant's answers in a small
  sample. Anything committed to the repository uses **aggregate counts only**
  (see §2, §3, and §6).
- The per-partner capture block in §6 is a **private, pseudonymised working
  record kept outside the repository**. Only redacted, paraphrased, aggregated
  evidence — with identifying combinations suppressed — is committed and scored
  with `docs/FOUNDING_DESIGN_PARTNER_DECISION_MATRIX.md`.
- This is discovery, not selling. The goal is to learn, not to persuade,
  pre-sell, or commit MYReSolve to any price, saving, or delivery date.

## 2. Facilitator safety guidance (read before every conversation)

### What must NOT be collected, entered into MYReSolve, or committed to the repository

- **No participant identities in the repository.** Do not record names, job
  titles tied to a named person, employers, email addresses, phone numbers, or
  anything that identifies a real individual or company in any committed file.
- **No confidential operational data.** Do not request or record actual KPI
  values, financial figures, customer lists, contracts, security details, or
  other confidential company information. Discovery is about *what matters and
  why*, not real performance data.
- **Nothing entered into the MYReSolve product.** MYReSolve does not yet have
  secure storage, consent, or account controls (`docs/EXTERNAL_FEEDBACK_001.md`,
  data boundary). Do not create accounts or enter any real participant or company
  data into the application.
- **Keep raw notes outside the repository.** Any notes that could identify a
  person or company stay in the facilitator's own private, access-controlled
  record and are never committed. Only anonymised, non-confidential summaries
  move into the repo.

### Claims boundaries (say nothing that is not approved and evidenced)

- **No savings or return claims.** Do not state or imply a percentage saving,
  cost reduction, ROI, or business result. The only value figure MYReSolve
  presents is the *illustrative* Estimated Annual Value at Risk shown with its
  approved qualification — describe it as illustrative, never as a promised
  saving.
- **No prices.** Pricing is not approved (`docs/SUBSCRIPTION_MVP_BRIEF.md` §9).
  Do not name, hint at, or anchor any price, discount, or trial length. You may
  explore *how* a leader would judge value and budget, not *what* it should cost.
- **No security guarantees, and no reciting of future controls as assurance.**
  Never promise that data is "completely secure" or "never shared." Also do not
  present planned protections (encryption, access limits, company separation,
  supplier handling) as things the product does today. Those are **proposed
  requirements that are not yet built or verified**; the approved trust wording
  in `docs/SECURITY_DATA_BLUEPRINT.md` may only be published once it matches the
  implemented, verified system and supplier agreements. If a participant asks
  about security, say accurately that the current free tool keeps assessment
  answers in their own browser, that secure storage and access controls are
  proposed requirements MYReSolve intends to build and verify before handling
  company data, and then **capture their concern rather than reassure it away**.
- **No roadmap promises.** Describe capabilities as *proposed* and *being
  explored*, not committed or scheduled.

### Neutrality rules

- Ask what the leader does and values; do not suggest the answer.
- Avoid leading phrasing ("Wouldn't it be helpful if…", "Don't you hate…").
- Let silence do the work; do not fill gaps with product pitches.
- Separate **evidence** (what the participant actually said or does today) from
  **assumption** (your inference) in every note — the capture fields in §6
  enforce this.

## 3. Selecting founding design partners (before any conversation)

Aim for **three to five** partners who can give honest, relevant signal. Record
the *criteria and a coded label only* — never names or contact details in the
repository.

### Selection criteria (score each candidate privately, outside the repo)

- **Relevant leadership role:** a CEO, COO, CFO, or equivalent who owns
  operational performance and improvement decisions.
- **Felt problem:** already tries to track organisational health, risk, or KPI
  performance and finds it effortful or fragmented.
- **Reporting reality:** has real source systems (BI tools, spreadsheets,
  operational systems) that hold their KPIs today.
- **Willingness to engage honestly:** will give candid, critical feedback rather
  than politeness.
- **Decision influence:** could plausibly adopt or authorise a tool like this if
  it proved valuable.
- **Diversity of context:** across the set, vary organisation size and sector so
  findings are not shaped by a single environment.

### Recording partners safely

**Re-identification risk in a small sample.** With only three to five partners
drawn from a known network, combining even "broad" attributes — sector, size
band, role type, and a distinctive fit rationale — can single out a person or
company. Treat the detailed roster as private, not repository content.

- **Private (never committed):** the full label-to-attribute roster and the
  mapping from label to a real person live only in the facilitator's own
  access-controlled record.
- **Repository (committed):** only a minimal count that shows the sample is
  reasonable — for example, "5 partners: 3 sectors represented; size bands
  small/medium; roles CEO/COO/CFO-type." Do **not** commit a per-partner row
  that ties sector + size + role + a distinctive rationale together.
- **Suppress combinations:** if any single attribute or combination would make a
  partner guessable (a lone sector, an unusual size, a one-of-a-kind rationale),
  generalise it or omit it before committing.

The Owner approves what aggregated evidence is retained (see §6 and the residual
-risk note in the task record).

## 4. The conversation (about 30–35 minutes)

Timings are a guide and sum to roughly 34 minutes before transitions; keep it
conversational and skip or reorder as the discussion flows. Each section lists
**neutral questions** and **what to listen for**.

### 0. Framing (2 min)
> "Thanks for making time. I'm trying to understand how leaders like you keep
> track of organisational performance and risk today. There are no right
> answers, nothing is being sold, and I'd value your honest and critical views."

Confirm they're comfortable, and that you will not record anything that
identifies them or their company.

### 1. Context and current approach (4 min)
- How do you currently get a picture of how your organisation is performing and
  where the risks are?
- What tools, reports, or routines do you rely on for that today?
- When you last needed that picture, what did you actually do?
- What works well about the current approach, and what is frustrating?

*Listen for:* the real job-to-be-done, existing habits, and the effort involved.
Evidence = what they do now; assumption = why you think they do it.

### 2. Reactions to the free assessment (3 min)
*(If they have completed it, ask about their experience; if not, describe it
neutrally as a free, point-in-time diagnosis producing a health score, department
risk overview, top risk areas, a confidence indicator, and an illustrative value
-at-risk figure.)*
- What stood out, useful or not, about that assessment?
- Did anything feel unclear, missing, or not credible?
- After seeing a result like that, what would you naturally want to do next?

*Listen for:* whether the free result creates enough trust and perceived value to
want more — without prompting them toward "subscribe."

### 3. The proposed ongoing outcome (5 min)
Describe the *proposed* subscription outcome neutrally: securely saving
assessments, repeating them over time, comparing leadership judgement against
actual KPI evidence (Current → Acceptable → Target), inviting colleagues, and
tracking whether things are improving.
- Which of those, if any, would matter to you, and why?
- Which would you not use?
- What would need to be true for something like this to be worth adopting?
- Where would this fit — or clash — with tools you already use?

*Listen for:* which capabilities are "must have" vs "nice"; the conditions for
adoption; substitutes they'd compare against. Do not ask for a price.

### 4. Buying context and decision process (4 min)
Explore *how* a decision like this would be made — never naming, hinting at, or
anchoring a price.
- If a tool like this proved genuinely useful, how would a decision to adopt or
  pay for it be made in your organisation?
- Who else would need to be involved or give approval?
- What evidence or proof would you need before committing budget to something
  like this?
- How do you usually budget for tools or services like this, and over what kind
  of cycle?
- Which parts of something like this would you expect to stay free, and which
  would you expect to pay for?

*Listen for:* the real buying process, the approvers, the evidence bar, the
budget cycle, and free-vs-paid expectations. Capture the process, not a price.
Evidence = how they actually buy today; assumption = your inference.

### 5. Optional expert support (3 min)
- When you face a significant performance or risk decision, do you ever bring in
  outside help? What kind, and when?
- Would facilitated interpretation of the evidence, a leadership alignment
  session, or a 90-day improvement plan be useful — separately from the software?

*Listen for:* appetite for consultancy as a *separate* value, and when in the
journey it matters.

### 6. Trust and data concerns (4 min)
- If you were to store organisational assessments and KPI evidence in a tool like
  this, what would you need to be confident about first?
- What concerns would you or your board raise about where that data lives and who
  can see it?
- What would build — or break — your trust in a provider handling this?
- Are there kinds of information you would *not* be willing to put into a tool
  like this at all?

*Listen for:* the specific trust blockers (security, control, company separation,
data ownership, exit/deletion). Do not reassure with guarantees; capture the
concern accurately.

### 7. Reporting needs (3 min)
- Who needs to see this information — you, your leadership team, a board?
- What does a genuinely useful report look like for that audience?
- How do you share performance and risk information today, and what's painful
  about it?

*Listen for:* the real reporting audiences and formats that would drive value.

### 8. Source-system priorities (4 min)
This is central to the low-effort data principle ("Connect once. Refresh
automatically. Focus on decisions, not data entry.").
- Where do your most important KPIs actually live today — which systems, tools,
  or spreadsheets hold them?
- Which of those numbers cost you the most repeated manual effort to pull
  together?
- If a tool could safely pull a small number of summary measures automatically,
  which one or two would remove the most work?
- Who controls access to those systems, and how hard is it to grant read-only
  access?

*Listen for:* the two or three connections that would remove the most repeated
reporting work, and the access/permission reality behind them. Capture system
*categories* (e.g. "BI dashboard", "finance system", "spreadsheet"), not
credentials or live data.

### 9. Close (2 min)
- If we built one thing first, what would make the biggest difference to you?
- Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Would you be open to a follow-up as this develops? *(Record willingness only;
  keep any contact detail in the private record, not the repo.)*

Thank them. Make no commitment about price, timing, or features.

## 5. Question hygiene checklist (run before finalising notes)

- [ ] No question assumed the answer or led the participant.
- [ ] No price, discount, or trial length was stated or implied; buying context
      was explored as a process, not a price.
- [ ] No saving, ROI, or business-result claim was made.
- [ ] No planned security control was presented as a current or verified
      protection; concerns were captured, not argued away.
- [ ] No real names, employers, contacts, or confidential figures were recorded
      in anything that will be committed.
- [ ] Before committing, identifying combinations were suppressed so no partner
      is guessable in a three-to-five sample; only redacted, paraphrased,
      aggregated evidence moved to the repository.
- [ ] Committed content uses aggregate counts only — no stable partner labels
      (A, B, C…) in any committed file; labels stay in private notes, and the
      Owner approved the aggregate before retention.
- [ ] Notes separate evidence (what was said/done) from assumption (inference).

## 6. Evidence capture

The per-partner block below is a **private, pseudonymised working record kept
outside the repository**. It exists so the facilitator can reason about a single
conversation; it is not committed. Only redacted, paraphrased, aggregated
findings — with identifying combinations suppressed (§3) — move into the
repository, and the Owner approves what aggregated evidence is retained.

Paraphrase in your own words; do not store distinctive verbatim quotations that
could identify a person or company.

### 6b. Committed aggregate (repository)

After each round, commit only a redacted, cross-partner summary — never the
private blocks above, and **never stable partner labels**. Use aggregate counts
only, suppress any small or distinctive cell that could identify a partner, and
have the Owner approve the summary before it is retained.

```

Number of partners contributing (count only, e.g. 4):
Valued outcomes — themes and how many partners raised each:
Buying context — themes (decision process, approvers, evidence bar, free-vs-paid):
Trust blockers — themes and how many partners raised each:
Reporting needs — themes:

Owner-approved for retention (yes/no):
```

These aggregated themes — not the private blocks — feed the customer-evidence
factors in `docs/FOUNDING_DESIGN_PARTNER_DECISION_MATRIX.md`.

## 7. What this guide deliberately does not do

- It does not contact anyone or schedule conversations.
- It does not set, suggest, or test specific prices.
- It does not change product code, assessment questions, or the locked scoring
  model.
- It does not authorise authentication, storage, billing, or real integrations —
  those remain gated by `docs/SUBSCRIPTION_MVP_BRIEF.md` and
  `docs/SECURE_SUBSCRIPTION_PILOT_PLAN.md`.
